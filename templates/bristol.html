PROCESS-BASED INTERPRETATION

How climate and water can affect rock, sediment, soil and ecosystems

01 · SLOPE HYDROLOGY

Rainfall → pore pressure → stability

PRECIPITATION → INFILTRATION → EFFECTIVE STRESS

Prolonged or intense rain can raise pore-water pressure and reduce effective stress. Response depends on permeability, slope geometry, discontinuities, drainage and antecedent wetness. The landslide register documents occurrences; it is not a probability surface.

02 · CLAY BEHAVIOUR

Wet–dry cycling → shrink–swell

SOIL MOISTURE ↔ VOLUME CHANGE

Moisture cycling can alter volume in susceptible clay-rich materials, influencing fissures, foundations and root-zone conditions. ERA5-Land soil moisture is contextual; mineralogy and local suction measurements are required.

03 · CHEMICAL WEATHERING

Water + temperature → reaction rates

WATER + HEAT + CO₂ → DISSOLUTION / HYDROLYSIS

Carbonate dissolution, silicate hydrolysis and oxidation depend on water residence, acidity, temperature, mineral surface area and biology. Climate series indicate boundary conditions—not measured weathering rates.

04 · FLUVIAL WORK

Discharge → erosion and transport

FLOW → SHEAR STRESS → SEDIMENT MOBILITY

River discharge is a proxy for hydrodynamic forcing, but channel geometry and sediment calibre control entrainment. Monthly flow conceals flood peaks and cannot quantify local erosion alone.

05 · BIOGEOCHEMISTRY

Soil water → oxygen and nutrient cycling

SATURATION → REDOX → MICROBIAL PROCESSES

Persistent wetness can limit oxygen diffusion and shift redox-sensitive cycling; drying changes microbial activity and plant water stress. Ecological inference requires field chemistry, vegetation and soil observations.

06 · FRACTURE & EXPOSURE

Thermal and moisture stress

CYCLING → FATIGUE / FRACTURE PROPAGATION

Thermal expansion, hydration, salt crystallisation and frost can weaken exposed material. Monthly temperature is insufficient for frost or thermal-shock attribution; higher-frequency site observations are needed.

CLIENT BRIEF · EXPLICITLY IMPLEMENTED

Three spatial levels, monthly evidence and North Atlantic circulation

01 · CITY / NEAR-CITY

Bristol

Monthly ERA5/ERA5-Land grid-cell context from 1950: temperature, rainfall, humidity, pressure, wind, evapotranspiration and soil moisture. These are reanalysis estimates, not a Bristol station splice.

02 · REGIONAL

South West England & Wales

Monthly homogenised HadUKP precipitation from 1873. This is the primary regional rainfall record for the commission and remains distinct from the Bristol grid cell.

03 · COUNTRY-SCALE

England & Wales / Central England

Monthly England–Wales precipitation from 1766 and Central England Temperature from 1659. These long records provide national and historical context, not local Bristol conditions.

NAO · MONTHLY 1950–PRESENT

North Atlantic Oscillation and South West rainfall

The NAO is shown graphically as a standardised circulation index together with standardised South West rainfall anomalies. It helps describe large-scale atmospheric co-variability; it does not deterministically reconstruct or attribute individual regional events.
